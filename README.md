# The Missing Premise in Baryogenesis

## The Scope of the Zero Antecedent

This repository contains an open examination of a scope assumption in the baryogenesis problem.

The observed universe contains a nonzero baryon asymmetry. Many baryogenesis mechanisms therefore investigate how such an asymmetry can arise from a baryon-symmetric antecedent.

This paper asks a narrower question:

**At what physical scope has the zero antecedent itself actually been established?**

The central distinction is:

Observed late baryon asymmetry does not, by itself, establish a zero baryon asymmetry on an earlier boundary.

A generation-from-zero burden is therefore live wherever the relevant zero antecedent has independently been established. A zero established inside one model or history family does not automatically extend to every physically admissible history.

The paper does not argue that the universe began with a nonzero baryon asymmetry. It does not select an initial condition, a baryogenesis mechanism, the Standard Model, new physics, or a realized cosmic history.

At the full history-class scope examined here, neither of the following has presently been established:

- that every physically admissible relevant history has a zero net-baryon antecedent;
- that at least one fully admitted relevant history demonstrably has a nonzero net-baryon antecedent.

The result is therefore a scope result rather than a replacement theory of baryogenesis.

## Falsifiability

The universal-zero claim can be defeated by one physically admissible relevant history that genuinely carries a nonzero net-baryon antecedent at the required comparison boundary.

Conversely, the generation-from-zero burden can be established across the full admissible history class if physics supplies an exhaustive result showing that every admitted relevant history shares the required zero antecedent.
